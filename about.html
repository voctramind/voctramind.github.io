ABOUT ME

Understanding Speech AI from the ground up.

I am at the verge of completing my PhD at IIT Madras. My work has taken me deep into Speech AI, deep learning, and representation learning, with my research primarily centred on speaker verification, speech representation learning, and efficient speech recognition.

But my learning has never been restricted to my thesis. I explore Speech AI broadly — following questions across different speech problems and the representations, learning objectives, and architectures behind them.

What I enjoy most is going underneath the model.

I don't rest at “it works.” I want to understand why it works, what makes it work, and what lies underneath it. That usually takes me back to the basics — mathematics, probability, geometry, optimisation, and the principles that shape an architecture.

These days, I spend my time learning, researching, teaching, and building around Speech AI.

Still curious. Still learning. Still going deeper.

MY JOURNEY

Deep learning needs non-linearity. Apparently, so did my career.

I started out in Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Anna University, graduating as a University Rank Holder — 29th rank.

In 2010–11, I prepared for GATE and secured AIR 273, which led me to my M.Tech at IIT Kharagpur.

After my M.Tech in 2013, I wasn't quite sure where I fit.

There was a stretch from June to December 2013 when I was figuring out what I wanted to do next. I joined NIT as Adhoc Faculty in December 2013, but that chapter lasted only until May 2014.

Then came another gap.

This time, I knew I had to actively do something — to keep learning, to keep moving, to hold on to something serious.

In February 2015, I joined BITS Pilani as faculty in Medical Instrumentation.

And that turned out to be an important turn in the story.

While teaching Medical Instrumentation, I found myself becoming increasingly interested in signal processing. Something about working with signals, systems and the mathematics behind them caught my attention.

That interest stayed.

By 2017, I had started preparing for IIT Madras, while continuing to teach at BITS.

In July 2018, I arrived at IIT Madras to begin my PhD in Signal Processing.

Sometimes, you have to travel to find your local minima.
THE PhD

A rather funny start.

I had a rather funny start to my PhD.

I still remember my first meeting with my supervisor.

SUPERVISOR
“Do you know coding?”
ME
“MATLAB.”

I laugh at that answer now.

It was really nice of him not to kick me out. 😄

At the beginning of my PhD, I discovered Transformers. I was fascinated by them and started wondering whether they could work well for Speaker Verification.

Self-attention could look across the entire utterance, allowing information from different parts of the speech to interact. Since speaker information is present throughout an utterance, I wondered whether this could help the model capture speaker characteristics more effectively.

My supervisor was leaning more towards ASR, so I spent some time convincing him to let me explore the SV direction. He trusted my instinct and gave me the freedom to see where it would take me.

At the same time, I began working on ASR, as he had suggested.

Somewhere along the way, I noticed another problem: Transformer parameters were increasing rapidly.

I started wondering:

Could I reduce the parameters without losing what made the architecture useful?

That question led me towards efficient ASR architectures — and became another important thread of my PhD.

My supervisor also gave us the freedom to fail, struggle and figure things out for ourselves. He would be there when we were stuck, but he didn't always pull us out of the problem.

You cannot learn swimming if someone keeps holding you up.

I think that shaped me more than I realised at the time. I became comfortable taking ownership of a problem, trying things that might not work, and staying with something difficult long enough to understand it.

That is probably where much of the confidence I have today comes from.

I don't need to know the answer before I begin. I trust myself to work my way towards it.

And after all these years, I suppose I am:

A pretrained model that can still be fine-tuned — without catastrophic forgetting. 😄
ANOTHER LAYER

One fine day during my PhD journey, I was assigned the task of training a completely new model: my daughter.

I was trying to grow as a researcher while learning to be a mother, and there were days when I genuinely felt torn between the two identities.

There were days when I managed both. There were days when one needed more of me. And there were days when I cried.

The pace changed. My curiosity for Speech AI did not.

WHAT IT TAUGHT ME

Motherhood changed the way I thought about time and attention.

I learnt that I could not do everything myself. I had to learn to delegate — not only in life, but at work too. I became better at deciding what genuinely needed my attention, what could be shared, and what could be trusted to someone else.

I also learnt to make the most of the time I had. Instead of waiting for long, uninterrupted stretches of time, I learnt to do focused work within a given window and make those hours count.

It wasn't always easy. I initially fought the situation, trying to make everything fit the way I wanted it to. Eventually, I learnt to accept the situation.

Acceptance, for me, has never meant standing still. It means understanding where I am, working with what I have, and moving from there.

I believe we are always at an optimum state — nothing better and nothing worse could have happened at that point in time.

But the next optimum depends on what we do at this moment.

This website is the result of my everyday wrestle with Speech AI — pulling ideas apart, chasing the mathematics, and asking one question again and again:

Why does this work?

It is my attempt to make that journey a little simpler for anyone else who worries about every why.
